This folder contains various ethical dilemmas discussed in class. Two main goals of discussing these dilemmas are:
1. to practice bringing descriptive clarity to such dilemmas, and
2. to recognize themes and patterns in ethical decision-making.

I have tried to surface dilemmas which are open-ended, rather than this-or-that. In some cases (e.g. good doctor), it seems important to start with a set of potential options, as the situation is rare/unfamiliar and may be difficult to consider. In cases where multiple options are added, I also include a "something else" option.

I have also made an effort to include "established" ethical dilemmas rather than coming up with new ones. Each dilemma has a source which references a movie, TV show, book, story, real-world event, etc.

This page is a work-in-progress. Here are six sources of moral dilemmas I have been reviewing:
1. [Some Moral Dilemmas — Friesian School](https://friesian.com/valley/dilemmas.htm) - large collection of classic moral dilemma examples. 
2. [Moral dilemmas — Humanists UK](https://understandinghumanism.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Moral-dilemmas-11.pdf) - short PDF with well-known dilemma scenarios.  
3. [Master Stimulus List of Ethical Dilemmas — UNC Greensboro](https://home.uncg.edu/~mjkane/files/Moore%20et%20al.,%20moral%20dilemma%20stim%20set%20for%20E-Prime,%20FINAL%20VERSION.pdf) - research-style set for moral dilemma studies.  
4. [Dilemma Deck for Discussion of Ethical Choices](https://www.siop.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Dilemma-Deck-For-Discussion-of-Ethical-Choices.pdf) — discussion deck focused on workplace and organizational ethics.  
5. [Ethics Case Studies, Society of Professional Journalists](https://www.spj.org/ethics-case-studies/) - case studies intended to be more realistic (it seems).  
6. [Moral Dilemmas, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-dilemmas/) - overview of the concept of moral dilemmas and related debates.
