# The Ring of Gyges

In Plato's *Republic*, Glaucon tells the story of Gyges, a shepherd who discovers a ring that makes its wearer invisible.

Glaucon argues that if there were two such rings, one worn by a "just" person and one by an "unjust" person, both would end up behaving the same way. He claims that no one is so virtuous that they would resist doing wrong, because they could be certain of never being caught or punished.

In Glaucon's view, people behave justly because they fear the consequences of injustice.

What do you think of Glaucon's argument? If you had such a ring, what would change?

Source: [Plato, *Republic*, Book II, trans. Benjamin Jowett, The Internet Classics Archive (MIT)](http://classics.mit.edu/Plato/republic.3.ii.html)  

See also: [Eric Brown, "Plato's Ethics and Politics in *The Republic*", The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/plato-ethics-politics/), on what Glaucon and Adeimantus demand of Socrates: "they want to be shown that most people are wrong, that justice is worth choosing for its own sake... regardless of the rewards or penalties bestowed on the just by other people and the gods."
