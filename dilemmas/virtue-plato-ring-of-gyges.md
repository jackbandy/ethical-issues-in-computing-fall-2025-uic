# The Ring of Gyges

In Plato's *Republic*, Glaucon tells the story of Gyges, a shepherd who discovers a ring that makes its wearer invisible.

Glaucon argues that if there were two such rings, one worn by a "just" person and one by an "unjust" person, both would end up behaving the same way. He claims that no one is so virtuous that they would resist doing wrong, because they could be certain of never being caught or punished.

In Glaucon's view, people behave justly because they fear the consequences of injustice.

What do you think of Glaucon's argument? If you had such a ring, what would change?

Source: [Plato, *Republic*, Book II, trans. Benjamin Jowett, The Internet Classics Archive (MIT)](http://classics.mit.edu/Plato/republic.3.ii.html)  

See also: [Eric Brown, "Plato's Ethics and Politics in *The Republic*", The Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/plato-ethics-politics/), on what Glaucon and Adeimantus demand of Socrates: "they want to be shown that most people are wrong, that justice is worth choosing for its own sake... regardless of the rewards or penalties bestowed on the just by other people and the gods."

See also: the sonar sequence in [*The Dark Knight*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dark_Knight) (2008), directed by Christopher Nolan. Batman converts every cell phone in Gotham into a sonar device so he can watch the whole city unseen. Asked "Beautiful, isn't it?", Lucius Fox answers: "Beautiful. Unethical. Dangerous." Fox agrees to use the machine once, on the condition that it destroys itself afterward, and treats its existence as his resignation. Where Gyges is a story about what a person does when no one can see them, this is a story about what a person does when they can see everyone.

See also: the [Tongan castaways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongan_castaways) (1965–66). Six Tongan boys, ages 13 to 19, were shipwrecked on the uninhabited island of ʻAta and survived there for fifteen months with no adults, no law, and no one watching. They divided the work in pairs (garden, kitchen, guard), kept a fire burning for over a year, set and healed a broken leg, and settled quarrels by having the two involved cool off apart. Rutger Bregman recounts their story in *[Humankind: A Hopeful History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humankind:_A_Hopeful_History)* (2020) as a real-world answer to *Lord of the Flies*. (If Glaucon was right about why people behave justly, the island of ʻAta featured conditions under which his prediction should have held.)
