Survey questions lightly adapted from [Phillip Rogaway](https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~rogaway/classes/188/materials/reader.html)'s "Basic Attitudes on Technology and Ethics."

* Computing has vastly improved the quality of most people’s lives.
* Technologically-created problems can usually be addressed with technological solutions.
* Computing itself is value-neutral: it is what people do with the computing that is right/wrong.
* It is impossible to accurately predict how a technology will be used, so it makes little sense to try.
* Technology is driven primarily by human desires, as expressed through the economic marketplace.
* There is a good chance that human society will collapse as a result of climate change.
* There’s a good chance that human society will collapse from a human-made disaster (nuclear war, biotech accident, etc.).
* Because of technological advances, I will probably live longer than my parents.
* I am generally optimistic about my future.
* I am generally optimistic about the collective future of UIC.
* I am generally optimistic about the collective future of Chicago.
* I am generally optimistic about the collective future of Illinois.
* I am generally optimistic about the collective future of the United States.
* I am generally optimistic about the collective future of humanity.
* The work of an engineer / computer scientist is inherently political.
* In making public policy decisions involving technology, we should rely mostly on the advice of experts.
* It is morally acceptable to work at a laboratory that designs, modifies, and/or tests nuclear weapons.
* It is morally acceptable to develop software for the guidance system of a "smart bomb" for the military.
* It is morally acceptable to work on oil-field technology for BP, Chevron, Exxon, or similar companies.
* It is morally acceptable to buy from technology companies that exploit poor workers.
* It is morally acceptable to eat beef, regardless of how it was farmed.
* There is no "right" or "wrong" in this world; everything is relative to cultural norms and/or personal belief.
* Most people are socially responsible most of the time.
* It's important to me that my daily actions have a positive influence on the state of the world.
* I know what kind of work I want to do after I graduate.
* The social impact of a company will be a factor in my decisions about where to work.
* I am morally obligated to follow the law, even if I do not agree with it.
* Education can improve someone's moral/ethical decision-making.
* Major aspects of my moral perspective are unlikely to change at this point in time.
* Most skills taught in the Computer Science major have been (or will be) automated
* Most decisions made by humans can be automated
* Ethical decision-making can be automated
